CREATURES / BEETLES

Beetles

Beetles you'll meet in the night forest, each with its own look and way of life. From dung beetles that rely on the Amami rabbit's droppings to rhinoceros beetles drawn to lights, here's a glimpse of the beetles found on Amami Oshima.

ABOUT

About This Field Guide

Amami Oshima alone is home to an enormous variety of beetles, many belonging to highly specialized groups, so this page adds species gradually, in field-guide format, as photos and information come together. Click a photo or name to see that species' full page.

Many of the beetles introduced here are endemic or rare species found only on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima, and collecting some of them is prohibited by law or ordinance. Please observe gently, without placing a burden on the creatures or their environment. This page also includes invasive species brought in from outside the island through human activity, which are marked separately from native species.
FIELD GUIDE

Beetles

―― Dung Beetles ――

Amami Hairy Dung Beetle
Scarabaeidae, subfamily Aphodiinae, Amami Hairy Dung Beetle Gilletianus atsushii (Ochi, 1986)

A rare dung beetle found on Amami Oshima, with very few collection records. It was described in 1986 by Teruo Ochi as *Aphodius (Trichaphodius) atsushii*, but later taxonomic revisions placed it in the genus Gilletianus. It is thought to live off the droppings of the Amami rabbit, but much about its ecology remains unknown, and records of it are limited.

Amami Mottled Dung Beetle
Scarabaeidae, subfamily Aphodiinae, Amami Mottled Dung Beetle Chilothorax ohishii (Masumoto, 1975)

An endemic species found only on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima. It was described in 1975 by Toshiya Masumoto as *Aphodius ohishii*, but is now placed in the genus Chilothorax. It feeds mainly on the droppings of the Amami rabbit, and is commonly seen from autumn through spring, while rarely appearing in summer.

Amami Round Dung Beetle
Scarabaeidae, subfamily Coprinae, Amami Round Dung Beetle Copris brachypterus Nomura, 1964

Body length 13–20mm. An endemic species found only on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima (a different subspecies occurs on Tokunoshima); its hind wings are reduced, so it cannot fly. At night it walks through mountain forests searching for the droppings of the Amami rabbit, and has the habit of digging a hole beneath a dung ball and dragging it in backwards with its front legs. Because its range is extremely limited, it has poor mobility, and past collection pressure has significantly reduced its numbers, it is listed as Vulnerable (VU) on the Ministry of the Environment's Red List.

Collecting prohibited (National Park Designated Animal, Ordinance-designated Rare Wild Species), Ministry of the Environment Red List: Vulnerable (VU)
Oshima Earth-boring Dung Beetle
Geotrupidae, Oshima Earth-boring Dung Beetle Phelotrupes oshimanus Fairmaire, 1895

Reaching 20mm in length, this is the largest species of Geotrupidae found in Japan. An endemic species found only on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima, it is active from autumn into winter and gathers at the droppings of the Amami rabbit and wild boar. It has the distinctive habit of cradling dung with its front legs and dragging it backwards into its own burrow. It is listed as Near Threatened on the Kagoshima Prefecture Red List.

Kagoshima Prefecture Red List: Near Threatened

―― Rhinoceros Beetles ――

Amami Small Rhinoceros Beetle
Scarabaeidae, subfamily Dynastinae, Amami Small Rhinoceros Beetle Eophileurus chinensis irregularis Prell, 1913

A subspecies of the small rhinoceros beetle *Eophileurus chinensis*, found on Amami Oshima, Tokunoshima, Yoro, and Uke. Males reach about 20–25mm and females about 15–20mm. It lives in forests from lowlands to mountains, and is nocturnal, often seen walking on the forest floor or along forest roads; from early summer to late autumn it also comes to sap and lights. Adults can be seen nearly year-round, though it is not a particularly abundant species.

Coconut Rhinoceros Beetle
Scarabaeidae, subfamily Dynastinae, Coconut Rhinoceros Beetle Oryctes rhinoceros (Linnaeus, 1758)

An invasive species native to the Indochina Peninsula region. The first record in Japan is believed to be its introduction to Ishigaki Island in imported materials from Taiwan in the early 20th century; it subsequently spread northward, and was confirmed on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima in 1991. It is now established and breeding naturally across nearly the entire Nansei Islands. It is known as an agricultural pest that bores into the stems and cores of palms, pineapple, and sugarcane, and there is concern about competition with native rhinoceros beetle species.

Invasive species (first confirmed on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima in 1991; a pest of palms, pineapple, and sugarcane)

―― Flower Chafers ――

Oshima Tiger Flower Chafer
Scarabaeidae, Oshima Tiger Flower Chafer Paratrichius duplicatus duplicatus Lewis, 1895

Body length 11.8–15.6mm. The nominate subspecies (P. d. duplicatus), found on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima; a different subspecies, the Okinawa Tiger Flower Chafer (P. d. okinawanus), occurs in the Okinawa island group. Compared with related tiger flower chafers, its wing covers are tinged darker, giving it a distinctive sheen. It appears from April to May.

―― Pill Scarabs ――

Tokara Pill Scarab
Ceratocanthidae, subfamily Ceratocanthinae, Tokara Pill Scarab Madrasostes kazumai Ochi, Johki et Nakata, 1990

A small beetle about 5.0–5.8mm long, found in the Tokara Islands (the type locality is Nakanoshima) and on Amami Oshima. It is distinguished by range from the closely related Sakishima Pill Scarab (*M. kazumai hisamatsui* Ochi, 1990), found in the Yaeyama Islands. It is considered a myrmecophilous species — found in environments close to ant nests, such as inside rotten wood or in the leaf litter layer around it — and the original description's paper title itself uses the word "myrmecophilous." When disturbed, it withdraws its antennae and legs into its body and rolls up into a perfect ball, like a pill bug, to protect itself.

―― Jewel Beetles ――

Amami Green Jewel Beetle
Buprestidae, Amami Green Jewel Beetle Chalcophora japonica oshimana Schönfeldt, 1890

Body length 24–40mm. A subspecies, found in the Amami and Okinawa island groups, of the jewel beetle *Chalcophora japonica*, which is widely distributed from Hokkaido to Okinawa. It favors dead or weakened pine trees, and the larvae feed and grow inside the wood. Adults are seen from early summer into summer.

Green Narrow Jewel Beetle
Buprestidae, Green Narrow Jewel Beetle Coraebus hastanus oberthueri

A small jewel beetle about 11mm long, found from Amami Oshima southward. Members of the genus Coraebus are known to be most active during the strong sunlight of summer, and this species too is seen from around June to September.

Azure Narrow Jewel Beetle
Buprestidae, Azure Narrow Jewel Beetle Coraebus niponicus Lewis, 1894

A small jewel beetle with an azure sheen, recorded on Amami Oshima. Members of the genus Coraebus are known to be most active during the strong sunlight of summer, and this species too is seen from around June to September.

―― Longhorn Beetles ――

Bates's Flat Longhorn Beetle
Cerambycidae, Bates's Flat Longhorn Beetle Eurypoda batesi Gahan, 1894

Body length 26–34mm. Widely distributed from Honshu (south of the Kanto and Chubu regions) through Shikoku, Kyushu, and the Nansei Islands north of Okinawa Island (including Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima), and also found outside Japan on the Korean Peninsula and in southwestern China. It gathers on camphor, chinquapin, and machilus trees, is nocturnal, and also comes to lights. Commonly seen from around June to August.

Ferrié's Red Longhorn Beetle
Cerambycidae, Ferrié's Red Longhorn Beetle Rosalia (Eurybatus) ferriei Vuillet, 1911

An endemic species found only on Amami Oshima. It has a beautiful red body marked with black patterns, and appears on standing dead trees deep in the forest around July. It is named after Father Ferrié, who collected the species on Amami Oshima. It is designated a Rare Wild Species under Kagoshima Prefecture's ordinance for the protection of rare wildlife, and its capture or collection is prohibited.

Collecting prohibited (Kagoshima Prefecture Rare Wildlife Protection Ordinance, Designated Rare Wild Species)

―― Leaf Beetles ――

Large Green Leaf Beetle
Chrysomelidae, Large Green Leaf Beetle Platycorynus japonicus

Body length about 10mm. A small leaf beetle with a metallic green sheen, found in the Nansei Islands (from Amami Oshima southward). It feeds on plants of the star jasmine group, and is seen from around April to October.
