Crow family (Corvidae), genus Garrulus. *Garrulus lidthi*

About 38 cm long. A species endemic to Japan found only on Amami Oshima,
Kakeromajima, and Uke-jima, beautiful with its azure-blue head,
wings, and tail, reddish-brown back, breast, and belly, and white bill and
wingtips. Designated a Natural Monument in 1921.
It prefers evergreen broadleaf forest and is omnivorous, feeding on
chinquapin and oak acorns. A year-round resident, it can be observed throughout the year. The breeding
season is from around February to May, when it lays 3-5 eggs in a tree
hollow or similar site; late February through early April, when
parent birds move about actively near the nest, is an especially good time
to spot one. Its numbers once declined due to
predation by feral cats and the introduced small Indian mongoose. Its status later improved, and in 2008 it was removed from
Japan's Nationally Rare Wild Species. In 2024, the mongoose was also declared
eradicated. It flies about actively during the day, but at night it can be
observed resting on power lines or branch tips along forest roads.
