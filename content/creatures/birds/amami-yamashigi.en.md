Sandpiper family (Scolopacidae), genus Scolopax. *Scolopax mira*

About 35-36 cm long. A species endemic to Japan found only in the Amami and
Okinawa island groups, with breeding confirmed on Amami Oshima, Kakeromajima,
Uke-jima, Yoro-jima, and Tokunoshima. It resembles the closely related
Eurasian woodcock but has longer legs, an overall blacker tone, and a
stockier build than other sandpipers. It prefers the dim forest floor of
evergreen broadleaf forest, is most active from dusk to dawn, and forages on
the ground for earthworms and insects. It becomes especially active in the
dim light of early morning and evening, and on moonlit nights. The breeding
season runs from around February to May, and at dusk males perform a
courtship and territorial "roding" display flight, making this the easiest
time of year to spot one. It nests on the ground within forests and lays
2-4 eggs from around March through May. Designated a Nationally Rare Wild
Species in 1993. Following the eradication of the small Indian mongoose and
forest recovery as logging has declined, its population has been gradually
recovering, and in March 2026 the Ministry of the Environment's 5th Red List
downgraded its status from Vulnerable (VU) to Near Threatened (NT). Predation
by feral cats remains an ongoing concern.
