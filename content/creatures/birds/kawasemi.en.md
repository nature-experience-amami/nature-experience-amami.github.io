Kingfisher family (Alcedinidae), genus Alcedo. *Alcedo atthis bengalensis*

About 17 cm long and weighing 19-40 g, a small kingfisher. The head and
back have a glossy blue-green sheen and the underparts are a vivid orange,
earning it the nickname "jewel of the water's edge." On Amami Oshima it is
a year-round resident found along rivers, irrigation channels beside rice
paddies, and ponds of many kinds. It watches the water from a perch on an
overhanging branch or post, then plunges in to catch small fish or shrimp
as soon as it spots one. Strongly territorial, it lives alone or in pairs
and digs a tunnel nest into a riverbank or eroded slope. The breeding
season runs from around March to August, and pairs may raise more than one
brood; courtship includes the male passing a small fish to the female,
known as courtship feeding, and the birds are especially active and easy
to watch during this period. Unlike most of the birds on this site, it can
be watched by daylight at the water's edge rather than in the night
forest.
