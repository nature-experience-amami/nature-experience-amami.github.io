Owl family (Strigidae), genus Otus. *Otus elegans elegans*

About 22 cm long, one of the smallest owls found in Japan. The whole body
is brown with fine, moth-eaten-looking mottling. It is a year-round
resident found from Amami Oshima southward through the Ryukyu Islands,
occurring not only in mature forest but also in woodland near houses.
Nocturnal, after sunset it repeats a low "koho, koho" call, and the female
sometimes answers with a "nya." It preys on insects, spiders, and geckos.
It nests in tree hollows, old woodpecker holes, or among the fronds of
bird's-nest fern, and lays 4-5 eggs during the breeding season from April
to around July. Only the female incubates, but both parents share the
work of feeding the chicks, and most fledge around July. Around the time
of fledging, chicks are still unsteady in flight and may be found perched
on branches close to the ground; rather than picking them up or shining
a light on them for long periods, it is best to quietly leave the area.
The call itself can be heard year-round, not only during the breeding
season.
