Hawk family (Accipitridae), genus Butastur. *Butastur indicus*

Males are about 47 cm long and females about 51 cm, with a wingspan of
105-115 cm; a medium-sized hawk. The head is grayish-brown, the upperparts
and breast are brown, and the throat is white with a black central streak.
It preys mainly on small animals such as snakes, lizards, and frogs, as
well as insects like cicadas and grasshoppers. It breeds in Kyushu and
northward through Honshu and Shikoku, and once breeding is finished, birds
migrate south from early September through the Nansei Islands toward
Southeast Asia. Amami Oshima is known as Japan's largest wintering ground
for the species, with the passage peaking around mid-October, when large
numbers of birds can be seen circling and rising together in what is
called a "hawk pillar." Many birds do not complete the journey south and
instead winter on the island; an estimated 2,000 or so stay from November
through around March. It is listed as Vulnerable on the Ministry of the
Environment's Red List and on Appendix II of CITES. Around the forests
used as roosts, avoid loud noises, sudden movements, and flash photography
that could startle the birds into unnecessary flight, and observe quietly
from a distance.
