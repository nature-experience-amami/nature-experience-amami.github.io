Kingfisher family (Alcedinidae), genus Halcyon. *Halcyon coromanda bangsi*

About 27 cm long, its whole body a glossy reddish-brown, with a pale-blue
patch on the rump that stands out clearly in flight. Locally on Amami
Oshima it is also known as "Kukkaru," and is loved for its far-carrying
"kyororororo" call. It is a summer migrant that arrives from its
Southeast Asian wintering grounds around the end of April, near Japan's
Golden Week holidays, and heads south again by around September. Despite
belonging to the kingfisher family, it eats relatively little fish,
preying widely on crabs, frogs, lizards, and insects of the forest. During
the breeding season from June to July it lays around five eggs in a tree
hollow or an old woodpecker nest, and the chicks fledge around July. Parent
birds become especially nervous during this period; approaching the nest
tree, making noise, or using flash photography can cause them to abandon
the nest or force the chicks to fledge early, so it is best to watch
quietly from a distance and for only a short time.
