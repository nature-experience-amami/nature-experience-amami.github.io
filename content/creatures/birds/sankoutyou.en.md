Monarch flycatcher family (Monarchidae), genus Terpsiphone.
*Terpsiphone atrocaudata illex*

Males measure about 45 cm including the tail streamers, females about 18
cm. The whole body has a black-purple sheen, and the eye-ring and bill are
a vivid cobalt blue. Males have tail feathers nearly three times the
length of the body, giving them a striking silhouette in flight. On Amami
Oshima it arrives as a summer migrant around April and, in the dim
interior of the forest, flits from branch to branch catching flying
insects such as small flies, wasps, and butterflies in mid-air. Its call
is said to sound like "tsuki-hi-hoshi, hoi-hoi-hoi" ("moon, sun, stars,
hoi-hoi-hoi"), which is why it is named the "three-light bird" after these
three lights. From May to July it builds a cup-shaped nest of moss in the
fork of a tree and shares incubation and chick-rearing duties. While
unfledged chicks are in the nest, lingering close by or shaking the
branch can cause the parents to abandon it, so it is best to watch briefly
through binoculars from a distance. It heads back to its Southeast Asian
wintering grounds around September.
