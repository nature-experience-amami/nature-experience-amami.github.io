Ibis family (Threskiornithidae), genus Platalea. *Platalea minor*

A large waterbird about 70-80 cm long. The whole body is white, and, as its
name suggests, it has a distinctive black, spoon-shaped bill and black skin
around the eyes. During the breeding season it grows a crest and its breast
takes on a yellowish tinge. It can be told apart from the closely related
Eurasian Spoonbill by the black facial skin, which extends broadly to the
base of the bill. It breeds on islands off the west coast of the Korean
Peninsula and the coast of northeastern China, and winters in Taiwan, Hong
Kong, southern China, Japan, and elsewhere. The world population fell to
around 300 birds in the early 1990s, but conservation efforts have driven a
recovery, and the 2025 international census counted more than 7,000. In
Japan, numbers arriving are on the rise, mainly in Kyushu, and on Amami
Oshima it too is observed on tidal flats and similar sites from winter
through spring. It was designated a Nationally Rare Wild Species under the
Act on Conservation of Endangered Species in 2020, prohibiting capture and
transfer (the Natural Monument designation applies not to the species
itself but to certain habitat sites within Kagoshima Prefecture). Around
2025 the IUCN Red List downgraded its status from Endangered (EN) to
Vulnerable (VU).
