Woodpecker family (Picidae), genus Dendrocopos.
*Dendrocopos leucotos owstoni*

About 28 cm long, a medium-sized woodpecker. An endemic subspecies found
only on Amami Oshima; the upper body is almost entirely black with white
spots, and the underside is a stronger brown than in other subspecies. The
crown is red in males and black in females, making the sexes easy to tell
apart. It lives mainly in mature laurel forest and excavates nest holes in
the trunks of large broadleaf trees. It feeds mainly on longhorn beetle
larvae hidden in rotten wood. Territorial proclamations and courtship
displays begin around winter, and from February through May its
"drumming" — rapid pecking at a trunk with its bill — and calls are easiest
to hear. In recent years its range has expanded across the whole of Amami
Oshima, partly due to the effects of pine wilt disease. Since the only
other woodpecker on Amami Oshima is the Amami pygmy woodpecker, the two can
be told apart by size alone. Designated a national Natural Monument in 1971
and a Nationally Rare Wild Species in 1993; capture and transfer are
prohibited.
