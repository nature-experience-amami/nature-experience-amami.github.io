Crow family (Corvidae), genus Garrulus. *Garrulus lidthi*

About 38 cm long. A species endemic to Japan found only on Amami Oshima,
Kakeromajima, and Uke-jima, beautiful with its azure-blue body, reddish-brown
wings, and white bill and wingtips. Designated a Natural Monument in 1921.
It prefers evergreen broadleaf forest and is omnivorous, feeding on
chinquapin and oak acorns. Nest-building begins around December, and during
the breeding season from around February through May it lays 3-7 eggs in a
tree hollow or similar site; late February through early April, when
parent birds move about actively near the nest, is an especially good time
to spot one. Its numbers once declined due to
predation by feral cats and the introduced small Indian mongoose, but
conservation efforts have paid off — including the 2024 declaration of
mongoose eradication — and its population has been recovering in recent
years. It flies about actively during the day, but at night it can be
observed resting on power lines or branch tips along forest roads.
