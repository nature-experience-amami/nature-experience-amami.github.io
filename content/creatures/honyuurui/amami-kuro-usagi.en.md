Rabbit family (Leporidae), genus Pentalagus. *Pentalagus furnessi*

Grows to about 41-51 cm and weighs 1.3-2.7 kg. A species endemic to Japan,
found only on Amami Oshima and Tokunoshima, and often called a "living
fossil" for retaining strongly primitive features. Designated a Natural
Monument in 1921 and a Special Natural Monument in 1963. Nocturnal, it bases
its activity around burrows dug in rock crevices or tree hollows, feeding on
chinquapin nuts and young shoots. Breeding occurs mainly in spring and
autumn, with one young born per litter. Its numbers once declined due to
predation by feral cats and the introduced small Indian mongoose, but as
mongoose eradication efforts have progressed, the population has been
recovering in recent years. It is the emblematic animal of night tours, and
it is not uncommon to encounter one hopping along a forest road.
